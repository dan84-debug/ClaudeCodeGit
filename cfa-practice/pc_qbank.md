### Q1 — Barboa Fund — Segmentation Approach [EQ-1]

**Context (EQ1):**
**Barboa Fund:** invests solely in the equity of companies in oil production and transportation industries in many countries.

**Question:**
The Barboa Fund can be best described as a fund segmented by:

A. size/style.
B. geography.
C. economic activity.

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:**

- **A is wrong.** The description says nothing about market cap or style (value, growth, blend). Size is usually market cap; style is scored on P/E, P/B, earnings growth, dividend yield, etc.
- **B is wrong.** The fund invests in *many* countries, so it is not segmented by geography. Geographic segmentation is by stage of development (developed, emerging, frontier markets).
- **C is correct.** The fund groups companies by what they produce and the inputs they use (oil production and transportation). That is segmentation by **economic activity** (the production-oriented approach).

**Key Principle:**
> Equity segmentation: **economic activity** (industry/sector), **size/style**, **geography**. Match the description to the dimension it actually uses.

### Q5 — Caribou Fund — Fee Type [EQ-5]

**Context (EQ1):**
**Note 1:** the fee on the Caribou Fund is a 15% share of any capital appreciation above a 7% threshold, with a high-water mark.

**Question:**
Based on Note 1, the fee on the Caribou Fund is best described as a:

A. performance fee.
B. management fee.
C. administrative fee.

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:**

- **A is correct.** A share of returns above a threshold, with a high-water mark, is a **performance fee**. These are usually "upward only": the manager earns when objectives are met and investors are not reimbursed when performance is negative.
- **B is wrong.** Management fees cover direct research and portfolio management costs and are typically a percentage of assets under management.
- **C is wrong.** Administrative fees cover corporate actions processing, performance and risk measurement, and voting at meetings. They are usually folded into the management fee.

**Key Principle:**
> Performance fee = incentive fee above a hurdle, usually with a high-water mark. Management fee = % of AUM.

### Q6 — DoGood Fund — Conflict of Interest vs Free Rider [EQ-6]

**Context (EQ1):**
**Note 2:** DoGood invests in Fleeker stock; Fleeker's pension fund has a significant investment in DoGood. The note says this creates a potential conflict of interest on the part of Fleeker, **but not** for the DoGood Fund.

**Note 3:** DoGood's PM has written policies stating the fund does not engage in shareholder activism, so it may be a free rider on other shareholders' activism.

**Question:**
Which of the following notes about the DoGood Fund is correct?

A. Only Note 2
B. Only Note 3
C. Both Note 2 and Note 3

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:**

- **A is wrong.** Note 2 is wrong. The conflict arises on the part of the **DoGood Fund**: it owns shares of a company (Fleeker) that is also an investor in the fund, so the PM may be influenced to support Fleeker's management to protect the mandate.

> Conflict of interest: the manager owns stock of a company that is also a client/investor. Free rider: benefits from others' engagement without paying for it.

### Q9 — Index Investability — Free Float and Buffering [EQ-9]

**Context (EQ2):**
Tong's statements on investable indexes:
- **Statement 1:** a free-float adjustment to a market-cap-weighted index lowers its liquidity.
- **Statement 2:** an index provider that uses a buffering policy makes the index more investable.

**Question:**
Which of Tong’s statements regarding equity index benchmarks is correct?

A. Only Statement 1
B. Only Statement 2
C. Both Statement 1 and Statement 2

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:**

- **A is wrong.** Statement 1 is wrong. Without a free-float adjustment, shares held by founders, governments or other companies inflate the weight and make the index less investable. A free-float adjustment better reflects actual liquidity; it does not lower it.
- **B is correct.** Buffering makes index transitions more gradual and orderly, which makes the index more investable. The three requirements for an index to underpin a strategy are rule-based, transparent and investable.
- **C is wrong.** Wrong because Statement 1 is wrong.

**Key Principle:**
> Free-float adjustment improves investability (reflects tradable shares). Buffering reduces turnover at index reconstitution.

### Q15 — Options Strategy for Income Without Cash Allocation [EQ-15] (Essay)

**Question:**
Describe an appropriate options strategy for Watson to present to the board.

**Model Answer / Explanation:**

A **covered call** strategy. The manager owns the stock and sells a call on it; income comes from the premium. It limits upside, which is acceptable if the shares are near intrinsic value and not expected to appreciate much.

**Key Principle:**

### Q18 — HHI and Effective Number of Stocks [EQ-18]

**Context (EQ4):**
Active Alpha holds 40 stocks. Cartwright wants a diversified vehicle that minimizes stock-specific risk relative to its benchmark. The **benchmark's concentration equals 28 equally weighted stocks**.

| Stock | Weight |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.074 |
| 2 | 0.068 |
| 3 | 0.062 |
| 4 | 0.048 |
| 5 | 0.031 |
| 6 to 40 | 0.717 |

Stocks 6 to 40 have a sum of squared weights of **0.01554**.

**Question:**
Given Cartwright’s preference, should his firm invest in the Active Alpha fund?

A. No, due to its HHI statistic of 0.0172
B. No, due to its HHI statistic of 0.0327
C. Yes, due to its HHI statistic of 0.0327

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:**

- **A is wrong.** 0.0172 is the sum of squares of the top five only (0.017209). It leaves out the 35 other stocks' 0.01554.
- **B is wrong.** HHI is 0.0327 and 1/0.0327 ≈ 31 effective stocks is *more* diversified than the benchmark's 28, so the fund meets Cartwright's preference.
- **C is correct.** HHI = 0.074² + 0.068² + 0.062² + 0.048² + 0.031² + 0.01554 = 0.005476 + 0.004624 + 0.003844 + 0.002304 + 0.000961 + 0.01554 = **0.0327**. Effective number of stocks = 1/0.0327 = 30.6 ≈ **31**, above the benchmark's 28. The fund is less concentrated than the benchmark, so invest.

**Flag:** Key's text for A writes the top-five sum as 0.172; the choice (0.0172) is the correct value.

**Key Principle:**
> HHI = Σ wᵢ². Effective number of stocks = 1/HHI. More effective stocks than the benchmark = less concentration.

### Q19 — What Shareholder Engagement Assists Companies With [EQ-19]

**Question:**
Shareholder engagement most likely assists companies:

A. in developing a more effective corporate governance culture.
B. to have full transparency in their disclosure of important information to all shareholders.
C. to remain focused on long-term decisions instead of deviating to achieve near-term earnings targets.

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:**

**Key Principle:**
> Engagement: pro = governance culture; cons = selective-disclosure risk and short-termism pressure.

### Q20 — When an Advisor Should Not Use a Strategy [EQ-20]

**Question:**
An investment advisor should not engage in:

### Q79 — Expected NAV of a Private Debt Fund [AL-20] (Essay)

### Q139 — Is Browne's Proposed Objective Appropriate? [IN-31]

### Q155 — Modified Duration of a Bank's Equity [IN-47]

**Context:**

**Question:**
An analyst gathers the following information about a bank's assets and liabilities:

- **Equity capital ratio for financial assets:** 10%
- **Modified duration of bank assets:** 2.0
- **Modified duration of bank liabilities:** 1.0

A. 9.1.
B. 11.1.
C. 11.9.

**Key Principle:**
> Equity duration = (A/E)·D_A − (A/E − 1)·D_L·(Δi/Δy). Liabilities are funded by A − E, i.e., (A/E − 1) × E.
